The version where a character finally owns things. Gear was the last part of a
build you had to keep on paper — a rifle, a suit of armour, a van — and it's now
a block on the sheet with its own budget, worn on and off as you play and rolled
like anything else. Alongside it, two things aimed squarely at the middle of a
fight: the whole window shrinks down to just the dice roller, and a GM can keep
each creature's important numbers pinned where they can see them.

## What's new

### Equipment, vehicles and gear

There's an Equipment block on the sheet. Gear is *chosen* from a catalog rather
than assembled — pick a weapon, armour or a piece of kit and it lands as a card,
grouped by what it is. It's bought in Equipment Points, a second currency that
comes from ranks of the Equipment advantage and never touches your power points,
with a budget bar that warns you when you've overspent. Click a card to draw or
stow the item; a worn weapon rolls exactly like an attack power, forcing the same
save on your target. Vehicles and installations are gear too — take a stock one
off the table or build your own, and a jet's flight speed shows up in your Speed
readout. You can also fit accessories to an item, so a scope belongs to the rifle
rather than floating loose in your inventory.

### Compact mode

Mid-fight the only part of the app anyone touches is the dice. So now the whole
window collapses to a small roller that stays on top of everything else — leave it
in a corner while you read the rules, or a map, or someone else's window. The
round button in the roller's bottom-right corner takes you in and out, as does
Ctrl+Shift+D. It's the same roller *moved*, not a second one, so a loaded trait,
your saved quick rolls and the table's shared history all come with it. If you
prefer it that way permanently, a setting pins it there.

### Pinned parameters on GM cards

Every player and NPC card in GM mode now carries a strip down its right side: the
four or five numbers you keep asking that creature for. Click one to load it into
your roller, double-click to roll it. They're live references rather than
snapshots, so they follow the character as it takes hits and switches powers on.
Drag to reorder, right-click to remove, and "+" browses everything that creature
has. A Settings page sets what new cards start with, separately for players and
NPCs, and can push those defaults out to the cards already on your board.

## Changes since 0.4.0

- **Added** the Equipment block — a gear catalog, an Equipment Point budget,
  custom items built in the same constructor powers use, buildable vehicles and
  installations, and accessories fitted to the item that carries them.
- **Added** compact mode: shrink the window to an always-on-top mini roller.
- **Added** pinned parameter strips to GM player and NPC cards, plus a GM Mode
  settings page for the defaults.
- **Added** a Quick NPC wizard — a usable creature from a name and four numbers.
- **Added** a connection indicator in the menu bar, so you can always see whether
  you're actually at the table.
- **Added** proper Trait / ABL / Rank / Total headers to Abilities and
  Resistances, which are now real tables like Skills.
- **Changed** how rolling works from the sheet: one click loads a trait into the
  roller, a double-click throws it, and a power's whole roll line is the button.
- **Changed** the lock toggle to sit on the menu bar, one click from anywhere.
- **Changed** popped-out blocks to stay above other windows by default, which is
  usually why you popped one out.
- **Fixed** sessions being dropped when a table went quiet, and reconnecting after
  a blip — you land back in the same seat, and a two-second Wi-Fi drop no longer
  takes the shared roll history with it.
- **Fixed** number and dropdown arrows that looked right but ignored clicks under
  the styled themes.
- **Fixed** washed-out, hard-to-read dialogs on the system theme.
- **Fixed** the Dice block jumping to a different height when you join a session.

## What's next

- Conditions that carry their own dice — recovery checks and turn economy.
- An initiative tracker.
- Groups of NPCs, for when the fight is against eight of something.
- A block editor, for arranging the sheet itself.
- Sample characters and a sample session, so the app can show what it does
  without you building something first.
- More mods, and more of the app opened up to them.
